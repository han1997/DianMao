# Product

## Register

product

## Users

This is a personal Android productivity app for the owner of the project. It is used to capture, organize, and review work without separating "tasks" and "todos" into different mental buckets. Users may be handling urgent same-day items, scheduled commitments, and long-running responsibilities in one place.

## Product Purpose

DianMao is a personal task-management tool inspired by TickTick, but organized around priority and timing rather than a strict task/todo distinction. Any item can live in a four-quadrant priority model, whether it is a short-term action or a long-term responsibility. Time-sensitive items should integrate with the phone system calendar and support alarms. Completed items can include a completion note, reflection, or lesson learned so the app becomes both a planner and a lightweight review log.

Success means the app feels fast enough for daily capture, clear enough to decide what matters now, and structured enough to preserve useful context after a task is done.

## Brand Personality

Simple, fluid, polished.

The product should feel calm and practical, with enough visual care that personal use is pleasant rather than utilitarian in a bare-minimum way. It should prioritize clarity, speed, and confidence over decoration.

## Anti-references

Avoid noisy productivity dashboards, gamified habit apps, bloated project-management interfaces, and designs that make the user classify every item before they can write it down. Avoid a rigid split between "todo" and "task" that does not match the owner's workflow. Avoid unusual Android controls that make the app feel less native.

## Design Principles

Capture before classification: adding an item should be quick, then details such as quadrant, time, alarm, and notes can be refined without blocking entry.

One model for different time horizons: same-day tasks and long-term responsibilities should share a consistent item model and four-quadrant organization.

Time sensitivity is first-class: calendar connection, alarms, and scheduled reminders should feel native and reliable, not bolted on.

Completion creates memory: finishing a task should support a short note or reflection so completed work remains useful.

Native familiarity, personal polish: the app should feel like a trustworthy Android tool with restrained, deliberate visual detail.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target normal Android accessibility basics: readable text, system font scaling, clear focus and touch targets, sufficient contrast, TalkBack-friendly labels, and support for reduced motion where motion is used. No additional accessibility requirements are currently defined.
